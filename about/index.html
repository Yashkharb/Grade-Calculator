About the project

A calculator that answers one question well.

GradeCalculator exists for the moment before a final exam when you want a straight answer — not a dashboard.

What it does

You enter three things: marks you’ve earned so far, how many marks were possible, and how many marks the final is worth. Then you pick your target overall percentage — typically the boundary for an A at your school.

The calculator tells you the minimum whole-number score you need on the final to reach that target. It assumes the current marks and the final are weighted equally by their point totals (the most common university pattern) and is transparent about that assumption.

Why it exists

Students often have to reverse-engineer a grade from a syllabus table or a spreadsheet that someone else built. That’s slow, error-prone, and stressful right before an exam.

This tool is intentionally small:

  • Fast — one form, one answer, no scrolling.
  • Private — calculations run in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server or stored.
  • Clear — rounding, edge cases, and assumptions are explained in plain language.

Who it’s for

Any student who knows their current fraction (e.g. 72/80) and the size of the final. It’s useful for high school, college, and university courses where the final is a single exam with a known point total. If your course uses category weightings (e.g. homework 30%, midterm 30%, final 40%), this calculator isn’t the right model — check your syllabus weighting instead.

Private by default

No account, no cookies for tracking, no marks ever leave your device.

Instant

Type, press Enter, see the answer. No page reloads or spinners.

Accurate

Validated maths with clear rounding — 89.2 becomes 90, not 89.

How the calculation works

Overall % = (earned + finalObtained) ÷ (possible + finalTotal) × 100

We solve for finalObtained:

required = ceil(target/100 × (possible + finalTotal) − earned)

If the result is ≤ 0, you already have enough. If it’s > finalTotal, an A isn’t mathematically possible and we show your maximum overall percentage. Otherwise we show the required marks and the percentage that represents.

See the FAQ for handling of fractional marks, weighting assumptions, and other grade boundaries.

No invented story

This is a small, independent educational tool — not a company, not a team, not a startup with a funding round. It was built to be useful, accessible, and honest about what it does and doesn’t do.
