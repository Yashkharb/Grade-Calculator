Frequently asked questions

FAQ

Short, accurate answers — organized the way students actually ask.

1 Using the calculator

What numbers do I enter?

Marks you’ve earned — your total so far (e.g. 72). Current marks possible — what that was out of (e.g. 80). Final exam total — what the final is worth (e.g. 100). Target — overall % you want (e.g. 90).

Same units throughout. If your course is out of 500, enter 360 and 400, etc. The calculator handles decimals like 72.5 / 80.

What does “current marks possible” mean?

It’s the denominator. If you scored 72 on assignments, quizzes, and midterms that were worth 80 together, you enter “72 earned / 80 possible”. It’s not a percentage field — it’s the point total your school uses.

Can I use decimals?

Yes. 72.5 / 80 with a 100 final and 90% target → 89.5 exact → 90 minimum. The required score is still rounded up to the nearest whole mark, even if your current marks include halves.

What happens if my final has a different maximum?

Enter whatever it is — 50, 75, 120. 72/80 + 50 final + 90% target → you need 45 / 50. The maths adapts automatically.

Can I explore “what if I score 80 on the final?”

Yes — after calculating, use the “What if I score…” slider. Drag to any final score and see your overall grade update instantly: 70 → 84.4%, 80 → 87.8%, 100 → 95.6% (example for 72/80 + 100).

How do I share my calculation?

After calculating, use Copy result for a concise message (great for WhatsApp/Discord) or Copy link for a shareable URL like ?earned=72&possible=80&final=100&target=90. The link restores the calculation client-side, but its values are visible to anyone with the link and may appear in normal server or analytics requests when opened. See Privacy.

2 The maths

How is the required final-exam score calculated?

Overall % = (marks you’ve earned + marks on final) ÷ (possible so far + final total) × 100.

We solve for marks on final:

required = ceil(target ÷ 100 × (possible + finalTotal) − earned)

The ceil is “round up” — you need the smallest whole mark that reaches the target.

Example: 72/80 + 100 final, target 90% → 0.90 × 180 − 72 = 90 → you need 90 / 100. Check: 72+90 = 162 / 180 = 90% ✓

What does “minimum marks required” mean?

It’s the lowest integer that still hits your target. If the exact maths says 89.2, 89 isn’t enough — you need 90. We show Exact: 89.2 → Minimum: 90 so you can verify. If exact is already 90, no rounding is shown.

Why does the calculator round up?

Most exams award whole marks. 89.2 → 90 guarantees the result is sufficient. We use a tiny epsilon (1e-9) so floating-point noise like 90.0000000001 doesn’t become 91.

Can I use this for grades other than A?

Yes. Change target to 60, 70, 80 — any value. Presets 70/75/80/85/90/95 are one click.

3 Edge cases

What happens if I already have enough marks?

You’ll see 0 / final total and “You already have enough for 90%”. We also show your overall with 0 (e.g. 52.8%) and your buffer to a perfect final, so you can see how safe you are.

What if an A is mathematically impossible?

We say so clearly, show your maximum possible overall with a perfect final (e.g. 86.1%), the gap in percentage points, and a table of achievable lower targets — each clickable to recalculate instantly.

What if I score 100% on the final?

Maximum overall = (earned + finalTotal) ÷ (possible + finalTotal) × 100. We show this for every result so you know your ceiling. Use the what-if slider to see 60/70/80/90/100 scenarios.

4 Weighting limits

Does this work with a 40%-weighted final?

By total points, yes — by category weight, no. This calculator assumes (possible + finalTotal) is the course total, so weighting is proportional to points.

If your syllabus says “final = 40% of grade regardless of points” (e.g. homework 30% + midterm 30% + final 40%), the model differs. You need a weighted calculator — coming soon — or adjust inputs to reflect weighting.

Quick check: Does possible + final equal your course total points on the syllabus? If yes, this tool is exact. If not, it’s an approximation.

What if assignments have different weights?

If your school weights categories (e.g. homework 20%, quizzes 20%, midterm 20%, final 40%), combine your current category grades into a weighted current average first, then use a weighted tool. This calculator will over- or under-estimate otherwise. We flag this next to the form — transparent maths builds trust.

5 Privacy

Are my marks stored?

The arithmetic runs in your browser and GradeCalculator does not keep a calculation history. However, the site may collect visitor usage data through Google Analytics. A shared link also contains its values in the URL, which can be seen by anyone you share it with and may be included in normal server or analytics requests. Closing the tab clears the form, but does not erase a link you already shared. See Privacy Policy.

Does the site track me?

No account is required. We may use Google Analytics to understand aggregate visitor usage, such as page views, interactions, device/browser details, and approximate location. Analytics may use cookies or similar identifiers. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts as a normal third-party web request. See the Privacy Policy for details.

Is the calculator free to use?

Yes — completely free, no account, no paywall.
